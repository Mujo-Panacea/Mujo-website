The MuJo shoulder system trains the shoulder over its full envelope of motion. It replaces subjective assessment with a structured record of every repetition, session by session.

<MediaRow image="/images/site/mujo-device-10.jpg" alt="MuJo shoulder rehabilitation device in a clinical setting">

## The mechanism

At the heart of every MuJo device is a patented multi-axial cam &mdash; a fully-moving, dual-plane loading system originally developed at the Department of Mechanical Engineering, Imperial College London. Where conventional cable and pulley machines constrain the user to a single plane, the MuJo cam moves with the joint through its natural, complex three-dimensional range.

- **US Patent 8,821,357** protects the core mechanism
- Dual-plane loading captures internal rotation, external rotation, elevation, and coupled scapular motion
- Designed to train the shoulder as the joint actually moves, not as a machine forces it to move

## The software (Mujo 1.5)

A patient-facing Android application, wirelessly connected to the device by Bluetooth, guides each session:

- **Real-time visual feedback** &mdash; the patient sees a live representation of their joint position moving through the prescribed range
- **Clinician-defined prescriptions** &mdash; sets, reps, ranges, and load pin positions configured per patient
- **Rep-level data capture** &mdash; maximum angle achieved, movement accuracy, and full movement traces recorded for every repetition
- **Cloud upload** &mdash; session results are uploaded to the MuJo backend for clinician review

Mujo 1.5 is a Class IIa medical device under the EU MDR route, currently in structured pilot with NHS and sports partners.

## Regulatory posture

- **FDA listing** under product code IKK (Class II)
- **MHRA registered** in the United Kingdom
- Development quality management aligned with ISO 13485
- Software developed under IEC 62304

## Where we are heading

The Mujo 2.0 platform, in development under a Knowledge Transfer Partnership, adds motorised assistance and integrated sensing. This unlocks:

- Assisted range-of-motion for early post-operative rehabilitation
- Higher-resolution kinematic data for the clinical dashboard
- Broader clinical indications beyond the current shoulder envelope

Roadmap beyond Mujo 2.0: hip, knee, and spine &mdash; applying the same multi-axial approach to other commonly injured, high-cost joints.
