## On the clinic floor

The station is built around a patented multi-axial cam. A cable machine holds the arm in one plane. The cam moves with the joint through its full envelope &mdash; elevation, internal and external rotation, and the coupled scapular motion that comes with them.

Resistance comes from a conventional mass stack. Nothing about the loading will be unfamiliar to your team.

- Trains the shoulder across its full envelope of motion, not a single plane
- Dual-plane loading through elevation, internal and external rotation, and coupled scapular motion
- Measures range of motion through the movement, not just at the end points

## What the practitioner sees

A tablet at the station runs the prescription you set &mdash; sets, reps, target range, load. The patient sees a live representation of their joint position moving through that range. You get the record.

- Range of motion at the shoulder and elbow, captured through the movement
- Movement accuracy against the prescribed pathway, repetition by repetition
- Rep counts, timing, and a full movement trace for every repetition
- Session results uploaded to the MuJo backend for review

It does not interpret any of that for you. It gives you a record you can put in front of a patient, a colleague or a commissioner.

## Where it is already installed

Twelve devices are in the field &mdash; NHS trusts, orthopaedic hospitals, elite sport institutes and private MSK clinics. The deployment footprint covers six practitioner sites.

- **Royal National Orthopaedic Hospital NHS Trust** &mdash; installed 2014, and the site of the 2016 clinical feasibility study
- **CircleBedfordshire MSK Service** &mdash; joined the Innovate UK connected-rehabilitation study in 2016
- **English Institute of Sport, Lilleshall** &mdash; installed 2015

Write-ups for each are in the [Evidence section](/evidence/).

## The evidence, and what it does not show

A clinical feasibility study ran at the Royal National Orthopaedic Hospital in 2016 and was published in *BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders* in 2018. Coventry University evaluated the devices in 2015. The RNOH and the University of Manchester were partners on an Innovate UK project on connected orthopaedic rehabilitation devices for remote monitoring after surgery.

A feasibility study establishes that a system can be run inside a clinical pathway. It does not establish that it beats usual care, and we do not claim that it does. The comparative trial work sits in the 2.0 programme and has not been done yet.

## What MuJo does not do

**It does not treat the patient.** You do. The station gives you loading you can control and a record you could not previously get.

**It does not diagnose or interpret.** The dashboard that trends session data over time is in development. What ships today records and reports.

**Mujo 2.0 is not on sale.** The motorised, sensor-enabled system is in development and is not available for purchase. It has not been assessed for conformity under EU MDR or UK MDR.

The installed device is registered with the MHRA in the United Kingdom and FDA-listed in the United States under product code IKK.

## Putting one in

The stations in the field run today as mechanical machines. The Mujo 1.5 release brings the tablet, the Bluetooth link and the data capture back on line on the existing hardware &mdash; no cable to the machine, no change at the back end.

Before you get near a procurement form, ask us for the things that actually decide it: the footprint and space requirement, what practitioner training involves, lead time from order to first session, and the data protection position &mdash; where session data is hosted, who controls it, and what you get back if you stop. We would rather answer those before a procurement questionnaire than during one.

- Private MSK and physiotherapy clinics carrying a shoulder caseload

The current system is specified for professional and clinical settings. It is not a home-use product.
